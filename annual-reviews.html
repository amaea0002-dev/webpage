Annual Reviews

The 12-month clock is always running.

FCA rules require advisers to conduct annual reviews for ongoing advisory clients. With hundreds of clients and multiple advisers, tracking every 12-month window manually is where breaches begin.

The requirement

What the FCA expects.

For firms providing ongoing advice, the FCA expects a documented annual review for every client within 12 months of the last review — without exception.

  • Review must be conducted within 12 months of the previous review date
  • Updated suitability report reflecting any changes in circumstances
  • Fact find refreshed to capture life changes, risk appetite, and financial position
  • Portfolio valuation and performance review documented
  • Consumer Duty outcome assessment completed for each review
  • Signed client acknowledgement on file

Missing an annual review is not a minor administrative lapse — it is a breach of FCA rules that exposes the firm to regulatory action and can invalidate the firm's liability protection.

The operational challenge

Why reviews slip.

No single source of truth

Review dates scattered across CRM, email, and calendar. No firm-wide view of which clients are approaching the 12-month threshold until it's already passed.

Incomplete documentation

The review may have been conducted, but the suitability report wasn't updated, the fact find wasn't refreshed, or the client acknowledgement isn't on file. Partial compliance is still a breach.

Adviser capacity

High-value advisers managing large books struggle to prioritise reviews without clear, automated escalation. Without visibility, the most overdue clients often stay invisible.
