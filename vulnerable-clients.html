Vulnerable Clients

Consumer Duty raises the bar for vulnerable clients.

Identifying a client as vulnerable is only the beginning. The FCA expects ongoing enhanced monitoring, documented fair outcomes, and evidence that your firm adapted its approach for each affected client.

The FCA definition

Who counts as vulnerable.

The FCA's Consumer Duty guidance defines a vulnerable consumer as someone who, due to their personal circumstances, is especially susceptible to harm. The four vulnerability drivers are:

  • Health — Physical or mental illness, disability, or addiction affecting financial decisions
  • Life events — Bereavement, job loss, relationship breakdown, or other recent significant changes
  • Resilience — Low financial resilience: over-indebtedness, low savings, or dependence on credit
  • Capability — Low financial literacy, language barriers, or cognitive decline

Vulnerability can be temporary or permanent, and a client's status can change between reviews. The FCA expects firms to re-assess at each interaction — not just at onboarding.

What Consumer Duty requires

Enhanced care means enhanced evidence.

Documented identification

Vulnerability must be formally identified and recorded — not just noted in a call log. The record must include the vulnerability driver, how it was identified, and the date.

Fair outcome evidence

Consumer Duty requires documented evidence that the outcome for each vulnerable client was fair. This is not covered by a standard suitability report — it requires a separate Consumer Duty outcome assessment.

Ongoing re-assessment

Vulnerability status must be reviewed at each client interaction. If a client's circumstances change, the record and any related documentation must be updated immediately.
