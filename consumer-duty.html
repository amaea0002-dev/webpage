FCA Consumer Duty

Consumer Duty explained.

What it requires from your firm, what the FCA looks for, and how to stay ahead of it without burying your team in manual evidence-gathering.

The regulation

Four outcomes, ongoing proof.

Consumer Duty (effective July 2023) requires firms to demonstrate good consumer outcomes across four areas — not just at onboarding, but continuously across the client relationship.

  • Products & Services — Suitable for the target market, meeting client needs
  • Price & Value — Charges are fair and proportionate to what's delivered
  • Consumer Understanding — Clients genuinely understand what they're getting
  • Consumer Support — Clients can access help when they need it

The FCA expects documented evidence of outcomes for each client — not a one-time assessment, but an ongoing paper trail that survives inspection.

What firms struggle with

The evidence problem.

Consumer Duty doesn't just require good practice — it requires documented proof, stored in a retrievable format, reviewable at any time by the FCA.

Scattered documentation

Suitability letters, vulnerability assessments, and fair value records spread across email, CRM, and SharePoint — impossible to audit in one place.

Vulnerable client gaps

Identifying vulnerability is only the first step. Consumer Duty requires ongoing enhanced monitoring and documented fair outcomes — for every affected client, every review cycle.

Annual assessment burden

The Consumer Duty annual board report requires data from across the entire client book — typically taking compliance teams days to pull together manually.
