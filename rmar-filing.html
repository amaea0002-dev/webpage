The RMAR: stop
dreading it.

The Retail Mediation Activities Return is an annual FCA submission. For most compliance teams it means weeks of manual data gathering from disconnected systems. Amaea pre-populates it automatically.

What the RMAR covers

The sections that matter most for IFAs.

The RMAR has multiple sections. For adviser firms, these are the ones that require the most data, and cause the most problems at submission time.

Section A: Firm Details

Firm registration, adviser headcount, and revenue breakdown by regulated activity. Generally straightforward, but mismatches with FCA Connect data trigger queries.

Section B: Complaints

Complaint volumes by category, resolution times, and redress paid. The most commonly incorrect section; advisers don't always log complaints formally, so data is unreliable at submission time.

Section F: Business Volumes

Policies arranged, funds under advice, and premium volumes by product type. Requires extraction and aggregation from your CRM (typically Intelliflo) for the full reporting period.

Section K: Consumer Duty

Vulnerable customer percentages and Consumer Duty outcome data. Introduced under Consumer Duty requirements and catching many firms unprepared, particularly around vulnerable client figures.

The most common RMAR errors

Errors submitted to the FCA can trigger a follow-up review. These are the gaps we see in almost every firm that prepares their RMAR manually.

  • ✕ Complaint data inconsistent with FCA's own Register; firms submit different numbers than FCA already holds
  • ✕ Business volumes don't reconcile to Intelliflo data; manual extraction introduces errors under deadline pressure
  • ✕ Vulnerable client percentage can't be verified; no single system tracked it across the year
  • ✕ Submission made late or with known errors because data gaps couldn't be resolved in time
