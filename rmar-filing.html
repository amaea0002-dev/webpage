RMAR Filing

The RMAR: what it is and how to stop dreading it.

The Retail Mediation Activities Return is an annual FCA submission. For most compliance teams it means weeks of manual data gathering from disconnected systems. It doesn't have to.

What it covers

An annual account of your mediation activities.

The RMAR requires FCA-authorised firms to report annually on their regulated activities across a range of sections. The key sections for IFA firms include:

  • Section A — Firm details, principal adviser count, and revenue breakdown
  • Section B — Complaints volumes, resolution times, and categories
  • Section C — Professional indemnity insurance details
  • Section D — Client money and assets (where applicable)
  • Section F — Business volumes: policies sold, premiums, and funds under advice
  • Section K — Vulnerable customer data and Consumer Duty outcomes

The preparation problem

Why RMAR takes so long.

Data lives everywhere

Complaint data in a spreadsheet. Business volumes in Intelliflo. Vulnerable client figures in a separate compliance log. The RMAR requires pulling all of it together under deadline pressure.

Section B surprises

Complaints data is the most common RMAR gap. Adviser teams don't always log complaints formally. By submission time, the figure either can't be verified or is wrong.

Last-minute errors

Errors submitted to the FCA can trigger a follow-up regulatory review. Many firms submit under pressure and then spend weeks responding to FCA queries about inconsistencies.
